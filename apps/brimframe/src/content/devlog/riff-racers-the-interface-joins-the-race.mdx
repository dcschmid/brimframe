Press Escape a moment after you cross the finish line in an older build of Riff Racers and the race result is gone. No fans, no record, straight back to the main menu. I found that in the audit that opened this sprint. The menus and the HUD were not ugly. In a handful of places they were quietly wrong, and in a racing game that sort of mistake lands on the player in the middle of a race.

Over one week in November I went through the whole interface of [Riff Racers](/games/): menus, HUD, controller navigation, accessibility options, results and Riff Rewind. That came to 156 commits.

Everything here happens in the first world, Metal, the one the demo is built around. The other music worlds I have written about before, in [Beyond the Finish Line](/devlog/riff-racers-beyond-the-finish-line-championship-career/), are still plans, not content.

## Audit first, redraw second

Before I changed anything, I measured. The audit found a mostly solid design and a list of behaviour problems I could put numbers on, so I fixed those instead of redrawing screens that worked. A layout check that measures real text rectangles inside the engine found no clipped, overlapping or off-edge text at 1280×800, 1280×720 and 1920×1080.

That is why the main menu looks exactly as it did, on purpose. What changed is how it behaves. With a series in progress, the menu now opens on Continue Championship, one press from the start. Quit asks before it closes the game, which costs four inputs instead of two, and that is the point.

## A kart that did not fit the frame

In driver select, all twelve karts were cut off by the right edge of the screen at 1280×720. Leo Cruz’s was the worst, 73 pixels out. Holding a direction piled up as many as twelve driver models at once. Now each kart is fitted from its own model bounds, at most two models are alive, and a BACK button lets you leave without picking anyone.

## Tracks and sets, written out

Track select now shows five tiles, each with a preview, a lock or trophy state and, new, the track’s name underneath. At 1280×800 the lock hint used to end in “unlocks Raz...”; it is a whole sentence now, “Top 3 unlocks Razorline Riot.” The 1920×1080 captures below show it complete in both builds. Difficulty has five cards from Warm-Up (three laps) to Legendary Set (eleven), each with a one-word character, and the selected card says SELECTED instead of relying on colour alone.

## Tyres before the green light

On the grid, the tyre choice could not be changed with any input device. The countdown wiped the steering before the choice read it, and a band across the middle of the screen hid my own kart. Now the forecast sits on the left, the tyre options and their grip on the right, my kart stays visible, and Enter or A starts the race. The image at the top of this post is that screen. I forced the rain with a developer switch for the capture; the weather system itself is the one in [When the Weather Changes the Race](/devlog/riff-racers-when-the-weather-changes-the-race/).

There is a price. When the forecast asks for a weather decision and nobody confirms, the grid waits up to ten seconds. On my machine, with a debug engine, the time from confirming Quick Race to being able to drive went from about 6.4 seconds to about 16.4. If you confirm, you never notice. Whether ten seconds is the right wait, or whether it should default to off, is still my call.

## The race still matters most

In the HUD, the time column moves aside from 10:00 so its digits stop covering their own labels, and the mini-map moved to the right and shrank, so it sits on the racing line far less often. In my line audit, five AI-driven runs of 130 seconds at 1280×800, the line ran under the old mini-map in 7.6 per cent of the frames and runs under the new one in 2.0. The pair below is the same seeded race at the same moment. It shows where things sit, not that they read better.

The tactical corner got more honest too. A pit tip now says it is optional and names the real button for the device in use, a gauge shows tyre wear, and the “stop requested” state, which never reached the advisor and so was never shown, now appears. I checked that in a staged scene and in a rain race with a stop at 1280×800. I have not driven a stop by hand yet.

None of this touches driving physics, the AI, scoring, the tyre model, weather or the save format. It does cost something: the race HUD grew from 535 to 564 GUI nodes, out of a budget of 1,024. On my laptop, in a debug build, I alternated runs of a build from the evening of 12 November and the final one on the same scene, and could not separate any frame-rate difference from the noise.

## Fewer interruptions between races

The championship hub now answers where I stand: my place, my points, the leader and the gap, and a route that says done, next race or later. The picture is a real saved series, with the player slot driven by the game’s AI through a developer switch, not a state I set up by hand.

Riff Rewind’s offer used to say only how many highlights it had found, and a single Enter or click ended the whole cut. It now lists them, with driver, lap and length, says that skipping discards them for good and that nothing is saved unless you choose it, and works with keyboard, controller or pointer. That is for solo races. A championship or co-op finish has no way into the highlight library yet.

## Built for a controller, checked at handheld sizes

Every menu path, from the title screen to quitting, runs with a controller alone. I tested that with a virtual gamepad on my development machine. I have not tried a physical pad or a Steam Deck, so please do not read any “verified” into it.

What it caught was small and annoying. Focus jumped back to the first row after Settings or Credits. Two held directions alternated instead of the newest one winning. A double tap across a screen change picked Quick Race or skipped the intro. The dialog that asks whether to swap two bindings opened on SWAP when the capture ended on a held direction. Focus now returns to the row you left, the newest direction wins, the double tap is dropped, and that dialog opens on KEEP BINDINGS. The full focus model is only in two places so far, the main menu and the pause confirmation; the double-tap guard reaches further.

## Accessibility, and an honest default

Accessibility has seven options now. HUD Detail is new. The six that already existed now take effect on every screen, where before the opening flash and the Credits slide ignored them. Both reduction options are off by default, and Reduced Motion alone does not remove the opening flash. Reduced Stage Flashes does. There is still no adjustable menu text size, no colour-blind mode, no screen reader and no subtitles. That is seven options where there were six, and it is nowhere near finished.

## Tried, dropped, and still open

I also built a runtime vector-font pilot for the HUD numerals, measured it against the bitmap font in isolation at three resolutions, decided against it and removed it again.

What is still open: two interface styles sit side by side, and the shared design tokens only reach the Controls settings so far. The co-op HUD has no map, callouts or pit and tyre panel. Quick Race and co-op have no driver choice. The grid wait is undecided. And everything I have said about controllers rests on a virtual gamepad.

If you would rather see it move, I cut a short tour of these screens and of races on all five tracks. It is real in-engine footage from a Linux debug build. The game’s own AI drives the player’s kart, the menus run on the virtual gamepad, and I forced the weather with developer switches: dry on four tracks, snow on Frostfire Run, rain on the grid and in the rain race. It has no music for now.
