I've been spending quite a bit of time on something I originally thought would be a fairly small addition to Riff Racers: the weather.

The idea sounded simple enough. Some races would be sunny, others cloudy, and occasionally there would be rain or snow. A little variety to make racing on the same tracks feel different from one run to the next.

Well, that turned into a much bigger job than I expected.

And I'm glad it did.

## A weather system that didn't want to rain

One of the first things I noticed during testing was that I could start race after race and somehow end up with perfectly dry weather every time.

Technically, a completely sunny race is supposed to be possible. That was always part of the plan. But when every race looks the same, the whole idea of dynamic weather loses its point.

So I went back to the weather system and spent more time on how conditions are selected, how they change during a race, and how those changes actually appear on screen.

The goal isn't to guarantee rain every few laps. Quite the opposite.

Sometimes the sun should stay out for the entire race. Sometimes the sky should gradually become overcast. Another time, the rain might start halfway through, get heavier, and eventually stop.

The important part is not knowing exactly what you're going to get when you hit Start Race.

## Rain needs to feel like rain

Getting the weather to change was only one part of the work.

At first, it was too easy to end up with weather that existed in the game logic but didn't make much of an impression while driving.

I wanted more than a few particles falling from the sky.

When it rains, the road should start looking wet. The surface gets darker and picks up highlights from the surroundings. Water sprays out behind the tires, especially when the karts are moving quickly or sliding through a corner.

And when the rain stops, the road shouldn't instantly become dry again.

That's one of my favorite details in the current version. The weather above you and the condition of the road are two different things. A race can continue on a wet surface even after the last raindrops have fallen.

It adds a little unpredictability without turning the handling into a guessing game.

## Snow is a different story

[Frostfire Run](/devlog/riff-racers-frostfire-run/) has been a particularly nice place to work on the winter effects.

Snowfall alone doesn't really sell the feeling of driving through a snowy environment. The road needs to look different, and the karts need to interact with it.

Now there's snow being kicked up behind the tires, visible trails across the surface, and more movement around the vehicles when they slide through a corner.

## It also changes how the karts drive

I didn't want the weather to be purely visual.

Wet roads and snowy surfaces affect grip, braking, and drifting. The idea is to make those differences noticeable while keeping the handling firmly on the arcade side.

Riff Racers isn't trying to become a racing simulator.

I still want the karts to be fun to throw into corners, even when the conditions aren't ideal.

The AI drivers also need to deal with those changing conditions. Some are more aggressive, some take a cleaner line, and others are more comfortable sliding around.

There's still balancing work to do here, especially with the full field of 12 drivers, but I like where this is heading. Weather gives the different driving styles another opportunity to stand out.

## Same track, different race

What I like most about this feature is that it adds variety without needing another track.

A familiar corner can feel different when the surface is wet. A snowy section looks completely different once several karts have driven through it. And a race that starts under clear skies doesn't necessarily have to finish that way.

Of course, sometimes nothing dramatic happens with the weather at all.

That's fine too.

I don't want every race to turn into a storm just because the game has a weather system.
