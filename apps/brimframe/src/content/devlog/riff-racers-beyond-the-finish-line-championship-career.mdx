An individual race can be fun and still leave an awkward question when it ends: what am I racing towards next?

With the [five Metal World tracks in place](/devlog/riff-racers-frostfire-run/), I wanted their results to belong together. A good finish should help with the next challenge. A difficult race should leave something worth recovering from. And finishing an entire world should leave a record you can return to.

That has become the next part of Riff Racers: World Championships and My Racing Career. The progression works in the current development build, including saving and resuming a championship. The presentation has also moved forward, although there are still some very visible gaps in the character assets.

## Five races, one world champion

The Metal Championship takes the same twelve drivers through Iron Chorus, Razorline Riot, Cathedral Circuit, Thunderforge and Frostfire Run, in that order. Cathedral Circuit is the track I introduced in [Into the Cathedral](/devlog/riff-racers-into-the-cathedral/).

You choose a driver and difficulty at the start, then keep both for the series. The points from all five races decide the champion. Winning the opener helps, but it leaves four races for somebody else to catch up.

I like what that asks of the tracks. Being quick on Iron Chorus matters, but you also need to get through the cathedral and bring a useful result home from Frostfire. Consistency has somewhere to show up now.

## Every position matters

First place earns 25 championship points, second gets 20 and third gets 16. The scale continues down to one point for twelfth. Every classified finisher earns something; a DNF or disqualification earns zero.

That gives a battle further down the field a consequence beyond the current race. Recovering from tenth to eighth is worth two extra points. It might not feel like much beside a win, but over five races those small recoveries can change the table.

The results screen shows the race classification and points together. The standings then show the running total, the latest gain and movement through the championship. I want the connection between the race you just finished and the series you are still in to be easy to read.

The test used ordinary driving inputs through the race engine, with full three-lap Warm-Up races and accelerated simulation time. It restarted the game after race two and checked the completed career again after reloading.

That checks a different question from whether a menu looks good: does the same championship survive leaving the game and coming back?

## Building a racing career

Each world has its own championship, while My Racing Career holds the longer view: career points, world titles, race wins, podium finishes and records by world and difficulty.

Career points use your best completed championship score for each world and difficulty. Finish with 100 points, improve to 115, then score 90 on another attempt, and that record contributes 115. Repeating a familiar championship cannot inflate the score endlessly.

Wins and completed championships still have their own counters. I wanted room for both kinds of progress: improving a personal best and keeping a history of what you have done. An unfinished series stays separate from those completed statistics.

Difficulty keeps its own records and trophy. Warm-Up runs for three laps, Opening Act for five, Headliner for seven, Encore for nine and Legendary Set for eleven. All five keep the full twelve-driver field.

The world records screen below belongs to the completed 63-point run. Third overall is a recorded achievement, but it does not award a champion trophy. Those distinctions need to stay clear when several attempts and difficulties sit alongside one another.

## Making the menus feel like part of the game

The early screens could show the information, but too much of the game disappeared between races. Lists of names and numbers needed some of the personality of an arcade kart racer.

The setup at the top of this article puts the driver cards beside a large animated character, with a biography and all five difficulty choices in view. World selection brings the five courses together. Results and standings give more space to the driver, the outcome and the next track.

There is practical work underneath that presentation: keyboard and controller focus, shortcuts between selection sections, and layouts checked in widescreen, 4:3 and ultrawide windows. Choosing a driver should be enjoyable without making starting the race laborious.

Seven drivers currently have their own presentation models. The other five cards explicitly say “3D pending”. Their identities and results work; their finished character presentation still needs assets.

## A proper championship celebration

After five races, I wanted a moment to let the result settle. The ceremony now has a real 3D podium, the top-three positions, concert lighting, confetti and a champion lifting the trophy.

Getting the cup to follow the character was a separate job from building the stage. In [Defold 1.14.0](/devlog/riff-racers-under-the-hood/), the trophy follows the animated hand bone. The lift and hold animations were prepared for each of the seven available character rigs, keeping the cup in the hand as the arm moves.

The trophy collection is working too. You can inspect an earned cup in 3D, while locked trophies explain the requirement. The championship is saved before the celebration starts, so skipping it or closing the game should not cost the result.

## One world today, more music worlds tomorrow

Metal remains the only installed world. The [demo plan](/devlog/riff-racers-road-to-first-demo/) is one world with five tracks. Beyond that, my intended scope is five worlds and 25 tracks for the full game, with another five worlds and 25 tracks as future DLC. Those additional worlds are plans, not playable content.

Each should have an independent championship feeding into the same career. A broader Grand Championship is a possible later step; it is not available now.

For the moment, I’m concentrating on making Metal hold together. The [racing AI](/devlog/riff-racers-the-grid-fights-back/) and [changing weather](/devlog/riff-racers-when-the-weather-changes-the-race/) give individual races more variety. Championship progression gives their results somewhere to go.

There is still character work and polish ahead, but the game is starting to hold a longer story than one trip to the finish line. That is a satisfying piece to have in place.

Thanks for following along as I keep building it.
